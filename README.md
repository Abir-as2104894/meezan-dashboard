# Meezan: Evaluating Cultural Relevance of Arabic LLM Benchmarks

This repository was created and published for the QCRI (Qatar Computing Research Institute) Summer Internship, under the project *Evaluating Cultural Relevance of Arabic AI Benchmarks: Assessing Alignment with Arabic and Islamic Contexts*, mentored by Hamdy Mubarak. The repository consists of Meezan, a single-file web application built to audit any Arabic Q&A or MCQ benchmark for cultural grounding, dialect coverage, and bias — a gap we identified in most existing Arabic benchmarks, which are rarely checked for whether they actually reflect Arab and Islamic contexts rather than translated or Western-centric framings. The tool's pipeline lets a user upload a dataset, map its columns (question, answer, category, organization), configure a judge LLM, and generate a full analytics dashboard with Excel/CSV/JSON exports — all without leaving the browser.

We evaluated two ready-made benchmarks with Meezan: Balsam (6,418 QA items from QCRI-affiliated organizations such as aixplain, NYU-AD, KSAA, and KAU) and PalmX (2,000 Gulf/Qatar-focused MCQ items). Both datasets were scored across twelve cultural and linguistic dimensions — including Cultural Coverage, Geographic Diversity, Dialect Diversity, Linguistic Authenticity, Cognitive Difficulty (Bloom taxonomy level), Fairness/Bias, Factuality, Realism, and Answer Quality — using an LLM-as-judge pipeline with GPT-5.1, prompted to return a structured JSON score and short justification per dimension for every item.

We found that PalmX scores noticeably higher than Balsam on Linguistic Authenticity (4.93 vs 4.21), Realism (4.49 vs 3.24), and Answer Quality (4.69 vs 3.82), suggesting it reads as more culturally grounded and less like translated or artificially-framed content. Both datasets, however, skew heavily toward Modern Standard Arabic with very little dialect representation, and most items cluster at the "Remember/Understand" end of Bloom's taxonomy rather than testing higher-order reasoning. Balsam's country mentions span well beyond the Arab world (e.g., US, UK, France), while PalmX stays tightly Gulf-focused. These gaps — dialect skew, low cognitive difficulty, and inconsistent geographic scope — motivate the next round of work: adding dialect-specific corpora, tightening the geographic-diversity metric to the Arab region, pushing benchmark creators toward higher Bloom levels (Apply/Analyze/Create), adding human validation of the LLM-judge scores, and building a public leaderboard for multi-model comparison.
